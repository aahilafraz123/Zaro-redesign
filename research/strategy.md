# Zaro redesign: research and strategy

## What Zaro is (one sentence)
A preventive blood test you order online, get drawn at Quest, and read in an app that turns
100+ lab values, your wearable data and a weekly check-in into one 0–100 score plus a daily
plan to improve it. No doctor visit, no insurance, FSA/HSA eligible.

## The product, in order of how a person experiences it
1. Pick a panel: Surface $149 (has something gone wrong?), Signal $249 (is something developing?),
   Source $399 (what's the root cause?). One-time purchase. Intro pricing until Nov 14, 2026.
2. Confirm a nearby Quest center by ZIP, pay (FSA/HSA card works), verify identity by email.
3. Fast 8–10 hours, bring photo ID, 5–10 minute draw at one of 2,250+ Quest centers.
4. Results in the app in 5–7 business days with plain-language explanations and PDF export.
5. Zaro Score (Optimal / Strong / Moderate / At-Risk / Critical), Biological Age and Heart Age (Source).
6. Recommendation engine (supplements, food, lifestyle tied to a specific marker) and an adaptive
   reminder engine that asks why when you skip, until the blocker is solved.
7. Retest every 3–6 months; the score becomes a trend line.

## What the current homepage does well (keep)
- Brand system: teal #069494, warm sand #F7F5F2, ink #1C1A17, sage and coral accents,
  Inclusive Sans display with a teal italic accent word, Quicksand body, DM Mono labels.
- The face video: calm, human, clinical-but-soft.
- Strong modules worth keeping: the score ring, biological vs heart age, percentile, trend,
  the notification demo, the three-question panel framing, the wearable marquee, the lab cards.

## What holds it back
1. The hero never says plainly what you get. The H1 rotates between abstract lines at 36px, and
   the real explanation sits in small grey body copy.
2. It explains the score before it explains the product. People need "blood test -> app -> score
   -> plan" first; then the score makes sense.
3. Jargon: "Intelligence Engines", "Corrective Action Plan Engine", "cascade model".
4. Pricing cards bury the best hook (the question each panel answers) in 11px mono text; the body
   systems are a dotted run-on line, so the tiers are hard to compare.
5. Fine print is inside the main steps (NY/NJ/RI restrictions in step 2 of How it works).
6. Every heading uses the same teal-italic trick, so it stops meaning anything.
7. Several demo modules render empty until an animation fires (wearable tiles read 0, the
   recommendation panel looks blank), which reads as broken.
8. No FAQ, privacy or clinician on the homepage, even though the objections (needles, cost vs
   insurance, privacy, "my doctor already does this") are exactly what stops a purchase.
9. Unforced accuracy risks: "150+ biomarkers" vs ~112 listed; Bio Age "Signal and Source" vs
   "Source only". The redesign uses "100+" and "Source" to stay defensible.

## Positioning
Headline promise: Know what's happening inside your body.
Mechanism in one breath: One blood draw. 100+ markers. One score. A plan that follows up.
Enemy: the annual physical that checks 12–15 markers and says "normal".
Proof: Quest CLIA labs, medical director, FSA/HSA, data never sold.

## Page architecture (AIDA)
- Nav: floating glass pill, 4 links + Get started.
- Attention: the promise, centered, with a 3D layered stage (face video + floating app layers)
  that un-tilts as you scroll.
- Problem: scroll-scrubbed statement about feeling off and the 12–15 marker physical.
- How it works: pinned four-step story with a phone that changes screens as you scroll.
- Interest: Zaro Score bento (score + bands, two ages, percentile, trend, wearables).
- Desire: "A report tells you. Zaro follows up." Interactive recommendation tabs and a
  reminder demo you can answer.
- Panels: three questions, three depths; clear "adds" per tier, side-by-side for easy comparison.
- Two paths: react to crisis vs compound your health (loss aversion, from the About page).
- Wearables marquee, FSA/HSA reframe, labs + privacy + medical director.
- FAQ: the six objections that stop a purchase.
- Action: final CTA, app links, footer.

## Psychology levers used
- Clarity before cleverness (5-second test: what is it, what do I get, what does it cost).
- Loss aversion and future self ("the 10-year-earlier version of every problem").
- Anchoring ($199 struck to $149, and "$400–800+ per round" to order these individually).
- Default choice (Signal highlighted as most popular).
- Mental accounting (FSA/HSA money you've already set aside).
- Endowment (show "your" score, "your" ages) and interactive demos that let people try it.
- Risk reversal and trust (Quest, CLIA, medical director, privacy, refund before draw).
